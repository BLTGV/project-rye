A memory for the business that keeps itself current.

Rye gives an organization one durable, searchable memory of what it knows — fed by the conversations, meetings, documents, and systems it already uses, and kept up to date by AI agents working inside boundaries people approve. 01

Executive summary

Most of what a company knows lives in people's heads and scattered apps. When someone leaves, or a decision from last quarter needs explaining, that knowledge is expensive to reconstruct — and sometimes gone.

Rye is organizational memory as infrastructure. It sits beside the systems you already run, preserves what happened as evidence, and records what the organization currently believes as accepted knowledge — with the full paper trail connecting the two. AI agents do the routine upkeep: noticing changes, filing updates, keeping the picture current. People stay in charge of what counts as true.

The operating rule is simple: people activate and revisit the boundaries; agents work inside them; anything unusual comes back to a person. Routine work does not require repeated approval, and no agent ever expands its own authority.

Where this stands: the memory itself — evidence, accepted facts, history, disputes — works today. The boundary engine that lets agents act alone on routine items is specified in this proposal and sequenced for delivery.

02

What the business gets

Nothing walks out the door

Knowledge captured from day-to-day work — who owns what, why a decision was made, where a deal stood — remains answerable when people move on.

Answers with receipts

Every accepted fact traces back to who said it, where, and when. No more "I think someone mentioned it in a meeting."

Routine upkeep, fewer meetings

Status changes, follow-up tasks, and record updates happen automatically when they match rules a person approved.

People judge the exceptions

New, sensitive, or conflicting items queue for quick human review — judgment where it matters, not clicks on everything.

Works with what you have

Plain PostgreSQL alongside your existing applications. No rip-and-replace, no new system of record to migrate into.

See how work really flows

Compare actual practice against official process — for compliance, and for finding improvements. Fairly: a missing record is treated as missing evidence, never as proof of wrongdoing.

Ask about any point in time

History is never overwritten. "What did we believe in March, and why?" is a query, not an archaeology project.

Disagreement is preserved

When two credible sources conflict, both claims stay visible until someone with authority resolves them — the memory never silently picks a side.

03

How knowledge accumulates

Six moves, in a loop. The essential idea: what people say and what the organization accepts as true are kept separate, with a deliberate checkpoint between them.

  1. Listen. After a person confirms a narrow scope and source boundaries, Rye watches the approved sources — team chat, meeting transcripts, email, documents, and connected applications.
  2. Remember. Evidence selected by the scope, classification, and retention policy preserves who said it, where, and when. It is not yet treated as accepted knowledge.
  3. Suggest. An agent reads the evidence and proposes what it means: "this deal sounds lost," "Niko agreed to update the safety doc by Friday," "this invoice contact has changed."
  4. Check. Every suggestion passes through the rulebook, which knows three answers: act on it, ask a person, or refuse. The rulebook weighs who spoke, what role they held at the time, what the official process allows, and how solid the evidence is.
  5. Accept. Approved knowledge joins the memory with its full paper trail — the evidence, the rule that allowed it, the person or agent that acted — and with effective dates, so plans and history stay distinct from today's truth.
  6. Learn. Proposed discovery tooling can notice repeated human decisions, draft a rule, and show its track record. A person with policy_set authority reviews and activates it. Review then shrinks to the exceptions.
A confident voice is not authority. Job titles, channel names, and forceful wording never change the memory by themselves — the rulebook checks who actually holds the pen, on that topic, on that day.
04

Start anywhere

Companies don't have their process written down — or they do, and reality drifted. Rye accepts both, in any order, and puts the difference to work.

Start empty — value on day one

Confirm one limited workflow, its source boundaries, and its retention and review policy. Then connect relevant sources. Evidence can accumulate before the official process is known, while every semantic promotion without a matching rule asks first. The resulting history can answer questions no single app could: how do deals actually move? who appears to decide?

Feed the official picture — whenever it exists

Load the org chart; import the process documents. They enter as authoritative candidates with provenance. An applicable source policy and an actor with policy_set authority must activate them; no observation period is required. Once active, they are still compared with observed practice, so stale documentation can be surfaced.

Compare — where compliance and improvement live

Once both pictures exist, their differences become a standing report, not an audit project: steps skipped in practice (a compliance question), rules nobody uses (process debt), decisions made by people the chart doesn't authorize (a stale chart — or a real problem). Each difference becomes a suggestion: fix the behavior, or fix the rule. Deciding which is — deliberately — a human call.

These three modes work independently, together, or in any order over time. Evidence accumulated before the rules existed is never lost. Rules loaded before any observation begin to govern only after activation and from their recorded effective time.
05

Under the hood

For the reader who wants the mechanism. Full contracts live in design/model/agent-driven-autonomy.md and the plugin pattern documents; this is the shape of it.

One decision, three answers

Every proposed accepted write — by any agent, through any interface — receives exactly one recorded decision, with durable reason codes and a snapshot of the policy that produced it:

SituationDecision
Complete match with an active, human-approved policyallow
No applicable policy (the day-one default for semantic claims)review
Speaker may propose but not decide this transitionreview
Unresolved identity, missing evidence or prerequisitereview
Contradicts an accepted claimreview via dispute
Missing capability or ungranted domain; non-overridable ruledeny

Authority is data, resolved in time

verified source identity → person → role held at that time → domain authority → permitted claim and speech act. Speech acts (reported, proposed, approved, decided, policy_set) replace any numeric rank: a salesperson may propose closing a deal; a manager must decide it; only a process owner may set policy. Role membership and delegation are temporal edges, evaluated at the moment the claim is about — not at ingestion time.

Observed and authoritative process, side by side

Process knowledge keeps two stances with shared comparison vocabulary and distinct assertion types: observed_process_transition (descriptive — what happened, with evidence) and process_definition / process_transition_policy (normative — what's permitted, set only by policy_set authority). The evaluator can only bind the normative lineage, by construction. Divergence between them is a derived view — unmodeled transitions, unobserved policy transitions, authority divergence — feeding compliance reporting and policy_change candidates. Neither lineage ever writes to the other, which is what makes the three on-ramps in §04 order-independent.

Three ways in, one contract

PathWhoEnforcement
Trusted SQLOperator-controlled agents on the databaseSession context, lifecycle helpers, audit — inside the trust boundary
Scoped DB / CLIAgents with restricted credentialsTransport role blocks table access; token-checked functions enforce capability, domain, and policy
Secure API / MCPExternal runtimesBearer tokens, allowlisted routes, the same policy-checked functions

All three converge on the same SQL contract; a behavior change lands once, in the database, not in three wrappers. Database roles never decide business authority — they only prevent bypassing the function surface.

The memory itself

  • Six domain-neutral core tables: entities, relationships, events, participants, time-versioned assertions, artifacts. Everything domain-specific is conventions and plugins, not schema.
  • Accepted facts are append-only and superseded, never edited — "as of" queries reconstruct any past belief and the rules in force at that moment.
  • Conflicting claims coexist as disputes until an authority resolves them; the losing claim remains part of history.
  • Compliance findings distinguish noncompliant from missing evidence; absence of a Slack message is never proof a step didn't happen.

Seven implementation gaps — implementation complete in draft

  1. Agent read boundary. implemented Draft PR #2 separates administrative routes and hardens scoped context packs. Stacked draft PR #4 adds temporal node-domain membership, complete multi-domain checks, token-bound database functions, and a transport role with no direct table access.
  2. CDC data protection. implemented Draft PR #3 captures public values plus classified-value digests, never classified values. Immutable legacy full-row events are admin-only and appear in a retention or protected-reingestion worklist.
  3. Secure knowledge reads. implemented Draft PRs #4 and #3 provide the same redacted, complete-domain search and summary contract through restricted SQL, HTTP, and MCP.
  4. Process-aware promotion. implemented Draft PR #3 atomically compiles accepted prior state, temporal process and authority, speech act, evidence, prior steps, and risk into explainable allow, review, or deny. Only allow applies.
  5. Reviewer agent tools. implemented Reviewer agents now have domain-filtered queues, adjudication, promotion, and governed transition evaluation and application through SQL, API, and MCP.
  6. Read-model freshness. implemented Dirty-on-write state, freshness gaps, maximum staleness, and manual, scheduled, or on-read refresh policies now govern CRM and PM read models.
  7. Documentation parity. implemented The website, human review UI, deployment guide, agent operations, CLI, data dictionary, conventions, security, integration, API, MCP, and function references now describe the executable surface.

Worked examples

The deal that wasn't dead — proposal, refusal, decision
Jordan · salesperson, in Slack

"Aurora is dead. We should close it out."

Rye

Preserves the message as evidence; files a closed-lost suggestion. The rulebook answers review: Jordan may propose this transition but not decide it, and the required loss reason is missing. The accepted deal stage does not change.

Maya · sales manager, next morning

"It's not dead — procurement moved the decision to next week."

Rye

Maya's role and decided speech act match the active transition policy. The suggestion is rejected, the deal stays open, and the whole exchange — including Jordan's message — remains in the memory as history.

The stale org chart — divergence doing its job
Observed

For two months, Priya has been approving discount exceptions.

Authoritative

The fed org chart says Maya holds that authority.

Rye

Reports an authority divergence — not a violation. It drafts two candidate fixes: update the chart (Priya took over in May), or flag the practice (Priya shouldn't be approving these). A person with authority over the org model picks one; either way the memory records why.
